Synaptic responses to neurotransmitter release

  • Action potentials lead to neurotransmitter release across the synaptic cleft, binding to postsynaptic receptors and causing EPSPs or IPSPs.

  • The PSP time course is often modeled as:

\[ \Delta V_{\text{PSP}}(t) = A t e^{-t/t_{\text{peak}}} \]

  • \(A\): amplitude parameter

  • \(t_{\text{peak}}\): time of maximum \(\Delta V\)

  • This is often called the alpha function.

PSPs as a change in conductance

  • In conductance-based models like HH, PSPs are modeled as a change in conductance:

\[ \begin{align*} \frac{d}{dt}v(t) &= \left[ g(t)(v(t)-E) - g_{leak}(v(t) - E_{leak}) \right] / C \\ \frac{d}{dt}g(t) &= \left[ -g(t) + A_{psp} \delta(t_{spike}) \right]/ t_{peak} \end{align*} \]

  • \(g(t)\): conductance
  • \(A_{psp}\): spike amplitude
  • \(t_{spike}\): spike time

Modelling PSPs in a simple neuron model

  • We switch from thinking of \(g(t)\) as a conductance to thinking of it as the concentration of neurotransmitter released by the presynaptic neuron.

  • Every neuron now tracks its own membrane potential \(v\), recovery variable \(u\), and neurotransmitter concentration \(g(t)\).

  • If neuron A projects to neuron B, then neuron A will release neurotransmitter \(g_{\text{A}}(t)\) into the synaptic cleft, which will bind to receptors on neuron B, causing a change in its membrane potential \(v_{\text{B}}\).

  • Presynaptic neurotransmitter release \(g_{\text{A}}(t)\) plays the role of external current in the equations for the postsynaptic neuron.

\[ \begin{align*} \frac{d}{dt} v_{\text{pre}} &= k(v_{\text{pre}} - v_{\text{rest}})(v - v_{\text{thresh}}) - u_{\text{pre}} + \mathbf{I_{\text{External}}} \\ \frac{d}{dt} u_{\text{pre}} &= a(bv_{\text{pre}} - u_{\text{pre}}) \\ \frac{d}{dt} g_{\text{pre}} &= \frac{-g_{\text{pre}} + A_{psp} \delta(t_{spike})}{t_{psp}} \\\\ \frac{d}{dt} v_{\text{post}} &= k(v_{\text{post}} - v_{\text{rest}})(v - v_{\text{thresh}}) - u_{\text{post}} + \mathbf{g_{\text{pre}}} \\ \frac{d}{dt} u_{\text{post}} &= a(bv_{\text{post}} - u_{\text{post}}) \\ \frac{d}{dt} g_{\text{post}} &= \frac{-g_{\text{post}} + A_{psp} \delta(t_{spike})}{t_{psp}} \\ \end{align*} \]
